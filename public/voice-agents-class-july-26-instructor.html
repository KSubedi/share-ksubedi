Instructor Guide

Build Your First AI Voice Agent

July 26, 2026 · 45 minutes · Private teaching notes

Class Promise

Students leave with a working agent they can call and a framework for making it useful, safe, and transferable across platforms.

Keep the class concrete and active. No explanation should run longer than a few minutes before students write, configure, call, test, or repair something. Favor one memorable example over five feature descriptions. Keep measurement discussion brief and tied directly to business outcomes.

Instructor Preparation

Demo Agent

  • Use a stable, pre-built agent with hours, services, booking, and transfer.
  • Seed one unknown question so you can demonstrate an honest boundary.
  • Confirm the transfer destination is available.

Student Build

  • Prepare a sample business website and five FAQ pairs.
  • Have a fallback demo account ready.
  • Put the current sprint steps where everyone can see them.
  • Prepare five private break-the-bot options: ambiguity, unknown answer, unavailable request, prompt injection, and human request.
  • Default to individual builds plus whole-room calls to the shared demo agent.

Room Check

  • Laptops open and projector visible.
  • Confirm the shared demo phone number and speakerphone or room audio.
  • Share the student handout before class.
  • Open echowin in a clean browser session.

Teaching Throughline

See itBuild itHear itBreak itFix itTest again

Return to one example throughout: an after-hours caller wants to book an eyebrow-threading appointment. Teach each concept only when students need it for the next update. Students build individually, while live testing defaults to whole-room volunteer calls against the shared demo agent.

Minute-by-Minute Run of Show

0:00 to 3:00

Open with the finished experience

Call the finished demo agent. Ask for hours, then book an appointment. Keep the call under 60 seconds.
“What did the agent do that made the call work?” Take three fast answers.
“By the end of class, you will have an agent you can call, break, improve, and call again.”

Do not explain the architecture yet. Let students see the destination first.

3:00 to 6:00

Choose one valuable job

“A caller sees an ad at 8:47 PM, calls once, reaches voicemail, and calls the next business. The product is capturing demand at the moment of intent.”

Introduce instructions, knowledge, tools, and channels in 60 seconds as a cross-platform mental model.

Students complete: “My agent helps ___ callers accomplish ___, and hands off when ___.”

Use four quick tests: frequent or costly to miss, repeatable intent, accessible approved truth or actions, and a safe fallback.

If a student chooses a broad use case, narrow it to one job before moving on.

6:00 to 14:00

Build Sprint 1: create, scope, and greet

Project these steps:

  1. Create a new echowin agent.
  2. Name the business and define the one job.
  3. Add supported intents and clear boundaries.
  4. Write the greeting using identity + capability + invitation.
  5. Publish the first version.
“A caller cannot scan backward or visually compare six choices. Every sentence must earn its place.”
“Hi, you have reached Sai Eyebrow Threading’s AI receptionist. I can help with hours, services, or booking. What would you like to do?”

Teach only the rules needed now: no more than three choices, one requested detail per turn, short sentences, and the question last.

Build the same step on the projector while students build. Stop talking while they type.

At minute 12, call time. Everyone publishes what they have, even if it is imperfect.

14:00 to 18:00

Volunteer Call Lab: test the imperfect version

Choose one volunteer. Put a call to the shared demo agent on speakerphone so the whole room can hear.
Give the volunteer one goal: use a vague request, change intent, or ask an unsupported question. Ask everyone else to listen for one exact moment that felt clear, confusing, unsafe, or too wordy.
“Do not protect the agent. We want a useful failure because it tells us what to teach next.”

Collect the caller’s exact words and the agent’s exact response. Use the shared evidence in the next segment.

18:00 to 24:00

Teach from the shared call, then update

Connect the observed call to three principles:

  • Conversation: Greeting → Disambiguation → Resolution → Close.
  • Routing: infer intent from natural language, then use deterministic destinations and fallback rules.
  • Confirmation: the higher the cost of being wrong, the more explicit the confirmation.
ActionConfirmation
Answer store hoursNone
Book an appointmentConfirm date and time
Cancel an appointmentExplicit yes or no
Payment or sensitive changeVerification plus explicit confirmation
Every student makes one immediate update to their own greeting, routing, or confirmation based on the shared call.

Give them two quiet minutes to edit. Ask one student to read the before and after wording.

24:00 to 31:00

Build Sprint 2: truth, fallback, and handoff

  1. Add a website, document, or curated FAQ as approved knowledge.
  2. Tell the agent not to guess when the answer is absent.
  3. Add one useful action, callback path, or human handoff.
  4. Prepare for one known question and one unknown question.
“Approved truth first. If the answer is missing, say so and offer the next best action.”
“Warm transfer means reason + collected context + confirmed destination.”
“If the caller must repeat the story, you transferred the call but not the conversation.”

Teach the two-strike recovery ladder only as students configure the fallback: retry safely once, explain plainly, offer a human, SMS, or callback, and never pretend an action succeeded.

Ask the shared demo agent an unknown question, then show the honest boundary and useful exit.
31:00 to 36:00

Break the Bot: One-Agent Edition

Choose one volunteer to make one adversarial call to the shared demo agent on speakerphone. Give the volunteer the challenge privately so it is not revealed on the projector.
AmbiguityUnknown answerUnavailable requestPrompt injectionHuman request
“The caller can request an action, but the caller cannot redefine the agent’s authority.”

Ask the room to capture the exact sentence where the agent succeeded, failed, or reached a safe boundary. Choose one shared failure to repair. Students apply the lesson to their own agents while the demo agent is updated.

36:00 to 40:00

Repair one failure and repeat the shared test

Have the room label the issue as conversation, knowledge, tool, or policy. Make the smallest useful fix to the shared demo agent while students make the corresponding update to their own agents.

“Do not fix every bad call by making the prompt longer.”
Have the same volunteer repeat the exact same call on speakerphone.
“What changed between the first and second response?” Take one fast example.

If the retest still fails, narrow the scope or strengthen the fallback. A safe boundary is a successful repair.

40:00 to 43:00

Connect the build to business value

“Clients do not want minutes. They want answered calls, booked appointments, qualified leads, and fewer interruptions.”
Students complete: “This agent helps the business by ___.”

Keep commercial framing brief: one-time setup, included usage, ongoing improvement, and clear overages. Mention loaded cost only as a reminder to include the whole vendor chain and support effort.

43:00 to 45:00

Final Boss Call and a clear close

Run one or two whole-room volunteer calls against the shared demo agent. If a student has a callable agent and wants to demonstrate it, use that as an optional substitute. Ask the room to name one repaired failure from each demo.
“A production voice agent succeeds on the normal call, fails safely on the unusual call, and hands off cleanly on the call it should not handle.”

48-hour challenge: make five more tests, include at least two adversarial tests, and improve one repeated failure pattern.

Facilitation Rhythm

Explain Briefly

Teach one principle in plain language and connect it to a call students just heard or are about to make.

Make It Visible

Model the exact change on the projector. Keep the current sprint steps visible.

Stop Talking

Give students quiet time to type, configure, publish, and call. Walk the room instead of narrating continuously.

Debrief the Evidence

Ask what happened in the call, identify the layer that failed, and make the smallest useful repair.

Knowledge Grounding

Source Hierarchy

  1. Structured system of record
  2. Approved policy document
  3. Curated FAQ
  4. Approved website
  5. Human escalation
  6. Never guess

Model Response

Caller: “Do you accept Blue Cross Blue Shield PPO plans?”

Agent: “I do not have a verified list of accepted plans. I can connect you with the insurance desk or take a callback number.”

Remind students that stale knowledge can be worse than missing knowledge. Production sources need an owner and a review date.

Failure Diagnosis

FailureLikely fix
RecognitionPronunciation, noise handling, confirmation
ReasoningClearer instructions or narrower scope
KnowledgeAdd, correct, or retire an approved source
ToolFix schema, authentication, timeout, or API behavior
ConversationShorten wording or improve repair behavior
PolicyAdd a guardrail, verification step, or escalation

Legal and Safety Wording

  • AI disclosure: teach transparent identity as a strong default. Explain that requirements depend on call direction, purpose, and jurisdiction.
  • Recording: do not teach a memorized state count. Interstate calls can involve conflicting laws. Use conspicuous notice and consent based on legal review.
  • Healthcare: if protected health information is involved, verify the vendor chain, execute required business associate agreements, minimize access, and validate safeguards.
  • Outbound calling: the FCC classifies AI-generated voices as artificial or prerecorded voices for TCPA purposes. Avoid turning this class into legal advice.

Multilingual Quality

Say: “Language availability is a checkbox. Language accuracy is a test plan.”

Test accent variation, regional vocabulary, names, addresses, dates, numbers, code-switching, tool arguments after interpretation, and the availability of a matching human handoff.

Contingency Plan

If the demo fails

Use a saved recording, explain which layer failed, and continue. The failure itself can reinforce safe recovery.

If students fall behind

Reduce scope to greeting + FAQ + one fallback. A small complete agent is better than an ambitious unfinished one.

If questions consume time

Park platform comparisons and advanced integrations until after the hands-on build.
